3. GPT zrobi najpierw ETAP 1 i się zatrzyma. Gdy obraz będzie dobry, napisz „ETAP 2” itd. Gdy coś trzeba poprawić, napisz to zwykłym językiem.
4. Każdy gotowy obraz pobierz (PNG, największa rozdzielczość) i wrzuć do tego samego folderu na Dysku, z nazwą podaną przy etapie.

```

The pictures will be ANIMATED ON THE WEBSITE IN LAYERS, so several images must have IDENTICAL framing and geometry. Work in STAGES: do ETAP 1 only, then stop and wait for my approval. When I write "ETAP 2", "ETAP 3" etc., do the next stage, always using the last approved house image as the exact base.

ATTACHED REFERENCE FILES (from my Google Drive folder "ChatGPT/Ojciec Goriot - dom"):
- 01_rzut_przekroj_obecny.png — the current schematic cross-section of the house used on the website. THIS IS A LAYOUT CONTRACT: clickable room areas are overlaid on exactly this layout, so the order, relative widths and floor heights of rooms must stay the same.
- 02_rzut_pokoj_goriota.png — a floor plan of Old Goriot's room with the furniture placed according to the novel. Follow it for room 5.
- 03_quint_pensjonat_od_ulicy.jpg, 04_quint_jadalnia.jpg, 05_quint_pokoj_goriota.jpg — illustrations by Jean-Paul Quint (1922, public domain): the boarding house from the street, the dining room, Goriot's room. Use them as references for MOOD, COSTUME, FURNITURE AND PERIOD DETAIL — not as the line style to copy.
If you cannot see any of these files, tell me which one before drawing.

GENERAL RULES FOR ALL STAGES
- ABSOLUTELY NO TEXT anywhere: no letters, numbers, labels, captions, signatures; the sign above the garden gate is blank; clock faces have no numerals.
- STYLE: hand-coloured French book illustration of the 1820s–1840s (fine ink line + transparent watercolour), refined and calm, muted historical palette (yellow ochre, faded green, grey-blue, brick red, brown), fine hatching, soft daylight from the LEFT. Not cartoonish, not a 3D render, not photorealistic.
- PERIOD: late November 1819, Bourbon Restoration Paris. Costumes and furniture strictly 1815–1820. No anachronisms (no gas lighting, no modern objects).
- Where the novel does not describe a room, keep it sparse and plausible; do not invent striking objects.
- Always deliver the largest square image you can.

LAYOUT CONTRACT (follow 01_rzut_przekroj_obecny.png EXACTLY):

- shabby, patched, respectable poverty: the higher the floor, the poorer the room.

ROOMS, FROM TOP TO BOTTOM (left to right as in the schematic):

ATTIC (under the slate mansard):
1. Christophe's cubbyhole (manservant): a tiny garret with a dormer and a straw pallet. Nothing else; the novel does not describe it.
2. The laundry-drying loft (wide, centre): bare boards, washing lines with white linen and napkins.
3. Sylvie's cubbyhole (the fat cook): a tiny garret with a dormer and a narrow bed. Bare.

10. (right of the stairwell) continuation of the second-floor lodgings, shutters half closed.

FIRST FLOOR (the two best apartments):

12.–13. Madame Couture's apartment (three rooms; she lives here with her ward Victorine; it was once refurbished for Goriot when he was rich): curtains of yellow calico, varnished armchairs upholstered in plush, a few poor paintings, cheap wallpaper "that a suburban tavern would despise"; a sewing basket by the window; two beds in the third room (right of the stairwell).

GROUND FLOOR (common rooms):
14. THE DRAWING ROOM (left, at the street end): a glass door to the garden and two barred windows onto the street; armchairs and chairs upholstered in horsehair cloth with alternating matt and shiny stripes; in the middle a round table with a grey-and-white imitation-marble top bearing a white porcelain tea set with half-worn gilt bands; a poor floor; wooden panelling up to door-handle height; above it a glossy scenic wallpaper with scenes from Fénelon's "Télémaque", and between the two barred windows Calypso's feast for the son of Ulysses (small classical figures in a landscape); a fireplace with a perfectly clean hearth (a fire is lit only on great occasions), two vases of old dusty artificial flowers flanking an ugly bluish marble clock.
15. THE DINING ROOM (next to the drawing room; the heart of the house): entirely wood-panelled, once painted a colour impossible to name, now layered with grime forming strange shapes; sticky shelves all round with chipped, clouded carafes, metal napkin rings and stacks of thick faience plates with blue borders; in a corner a box with numbered pigeon-holes for wine-stained napkins (no visible numbers); a barometer with a little Capuchin figure that comes out when it rains; ugly engravings in black lacquered frames with a gold fillet; a tortoiseshell clock inlaid with brass; a green stove; oil lamps in wall sconces where dust mixes with oil; a LONG TABLE covered with greasy oilcloth, set for eighteen; rickety chairs; wretched curled mats of sea-grass; old foot-warmers with broken holes; a red-tiled floor full of bumps.
16. THE ENTRANCE HALL between the dining room and the kitchen: the painted and waxed wooden staircase starts here (the stairwell column), with the door to the garden.
17. THE KITCHEN (right): stone sink, copper pans, a hearth, a window onto the back yard. The novel does not describe the kitchen; keep it plain and plausible.

GARDEN STRIP (bottom foreground, as wide as the house): along the facade a paved gutter, then a sandy path lined with geraniums, oleanders and pomegranate shrubs in large blue-and-white faience pots; a small garden gate with a bell and a blank sign; on the wall facing the street a niche painted as green marble with a plaster statue of Cupid whose varnish is peeling; ivy hanging from the neighbouring wall; espaliers and a dusty vine on the garden wall; a little arbour of lime trees with a round green-painted table and benches; beds of artichokes, sorrel, lettuce and parsley.

MOOD: cold, shabby, decline toward the top; late autumn light.

Before drawing, briefly confirm (in 3–4 lines) that you can see the five reference files and that you will keep the layout of file 01. Then draw ETAP 1 and stop.
```
